Photosynthesis

How plants turn light into sugar · Dr. Maya Okafor

The big picture

One equation to remember

6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O — light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂
  • Photosynthesis stores light energy as chemical energy in sugar (glucose).
  • It happens in the chloroplasts of plant cells and algae.
  • It is roughly the reverse of cellular respiration.
Where it happens

Inside the chloroplast

Thylakoids

Flattened sacs stacked into grana. Their membranes hold chlorophyll. The light reactions happen here.

Stroma

The fluid around the thylakoids. The Calvin cycle happens here.
Stage 1

The light reactions (thylakoid membranes)

  • Chlorophyll in photosystem II and photosystem I absorbs light.
  • Water is split: this is where the oxygen we breathe comes from.
  • The energy makes ATP and NADPH, carried to the Calvin cycle.
  • Needs light. Produces O₂, ATP and NADPH.
Stage 2

The Calvin cycle (stroma)

  • Carbon fixation: the enzyme RuBisCO attaches CO₂ to RuBP.
  • Reduction: ATP and NADPH turn the products into G3P, a 3-carbon sugar.
  • Regeneration: most G3P is recycled to rebuild RuBP.
  • 3 turns of the cycle make 1 G3P to export, using 9 ATP and 6 NADPH.

Also called the light-independent reactions. It doesn't need light directly, but it depends on the ATP and NADPH the light reactions make.

Adaptations

C₃, C₄ and CAM plants

C₃

Most plants (wheat, rice). Fix CO₂ straight into the Calvin cycle. Lose water on hot days.

C₄

Corn, sugarcane. Fix CO₂ in one cell, run the Calvin cycle in another. Efficient in heat.

CAM

Cacti, pineapple. Open stomata at night, store CO₂, and fix it by day. Save water.
Lab preview

What changes the rate of photosynthesis?

  • Light intensity: more light, faster, until it levels off.
  • CO₂ concentration: more CO₂, faster, until it levels off.
  • Temperature: enzymes like RuBisCO work best in a middle range.
