What this course is about

BIO 101 is a first look at how living things work, from the molecules inside a single cell to the way energy moves through an ecosystem. We spend most of the semester on cells: how they are built, how they get and use energy, how they copy themselves, and how traits pass from parents to offspring.

By the end of the course you will be able to

  • Describe the structure of a eukaryotic cell and what each organelle does.
  • Explain how photosynthesis captures light energy and stores it in sugar.
  • Explain how cellular respiration releases that energy as ATP.
  • Compare mitosis and meiosis, and predict simple patterns of inheritance.

Exams

Midterm 2 is Thursday, November 12, in Science Hall 204, during the regular lecture time (9:30–10:45 AM). It covers Weeks 6–10: energy and enzymes, cellular respiration, and photosynthesis. Expect about 30 multiple-choice questions and two short-answer questions. One short-answer question will ask you to compare photosynthesis and cellular respiration: where each happens, what goes in, and what comes out.

Bring a pencil and your student ID. No notes or calculators.

Each midterm is worth 20% of your grade. The final exam is cumulative and worth 30%. If your final exam score is higher than your lowest midterm score, it replaces that midterm.

  • Space out your studying. Twenty minutes a day beats three hours the night before.
