# The demo video: script and shot list

2:56, for the hackathon's Devpost page and YouTube (under 3 minutes, public, in English). Everything on screen is the real product: build-a-bot.site in a browser, the real desktop app, and a real call through the voice server. The promo (`video/STORYBOARD.md`) sells the idea in a minute; this one shows it working. The cut is in `edit.mjs`; how it's made is in `README.md`.

Diego asked for it to start on the landing page and the recipes, then sign in and build a bot: every brain there is (the providers for your own key and all the open models on Nebius), Gmail, Google Docs and Meeting notes picked by hand, no stopwatch on screen, and the email sign-in, voice typing and the meeting kept. Then, for this version: "this needs to be really good we need to really sell this to the judges. this should be the absolute best and final version", with the narration ours. He's **Diego** in the video; his bot is **Juno** (Nimbus the cloud, in grape).

**Narrator:** a synthesized voice (ElevenLabs, "Justin Case"), the whole script read in one take, then cut into sentences by `vo/split.py`. The brain step's three lines (S07 to S09) are a second take, from when Diego asked for them to be about the open models and your data instead of your own API key. The lines, in order, are `vo/lines.json`. Diego can read them himself instead (`README.md`).

**What the hackathon asks for:** under 3 minutes, public on YouTube, the app working as shown, and how it uses Nebius and NVIDIA. The video names each aloud and on screen: NVIDIA Nemotron on Nebius Token Factory (the thinking), NVIDIA Parakeet and Magpie (hearing and speaking, on our voice server, and Parakeet on your computer too), and Tavily (web search). The judges score Technological Implementation, Design, Potential Impact and Quality of the Idea.

## Every claim, and where it's true

- **Recorded on 2026-10-28 and 29** on build-a-bot.site. The website, the sign-in, the builder, the files, the web search and the call are one fresh account, signed in by email (`record/site.mjs`), and its Juno. The desktop scenes and the notes page are from the day before: the desktop app from `desktop/`, paired to the first demo account and its Juno, built the same way.
- **"In about a minute":** the builder take is 54 seconds, start to finish. The edit speeds up typing the name, the look at your own API key and the "Bringing Juno to life" wait, cuts the app's own scroll back to the top between two steps, and holds still moments (the cursor resting) where the narration needs them.
- **"12 open models on Nebius Token Factory … at cost":** the brain step lists all 12, three of them NVIDIA Nemotron, each with its price a message; the prices are Token Factory's, with nothing added. "Every account starts with $5.00 of credit, no card needed" is on the same step.
- **"Juno gets smarter the more you use it":** memory, not training. A bot keeps up to 2,000 memories and brings the ones that matter into each message, on any brain (`src/lib/memory/index.ts`); the promo makes the same claim. No model is trained on anyone's chats.
- **"Your data stays yours":** never sold and never used to train AI models (the brain step: "Your chats aren't used to train models"; the Account page: "Your chats, memories and files aren't used to train AI models"), and everything can be exported or deleted from the Account page.
- **"Zero data retention":** on for the included open models since 2026-10-24, in our Nebius Token Factory organization's settings, so Token Factory keeps none of their prompts and replies (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "zero data retention is on"). A bot on its own API key follows that provider's terms, which is why the line is said over the open models.
- **"Over a hundred" apps:** the apps step's catalog says "All 116", and takes any MCP server by its URL.
- **"Ask me first … enforced in code":** the policy engine (`src/lib/policy/engine.ts`), not the prompt.
- **Files:** read once into passages with their page; the photo of notes through a vision model on Token Factory; each flashcard keeps its source. The class files are made up for the video (`props/`): BIO 101 and its syllabus aren't real. Juno's reply lists "C₃/C₄/C₄ plant adaptations" among the topics (it means C₃, C₄ and CAM plants): left as it came out.
- **Web search:** Tavily, on for every bot; each lookup costs 0.8¢ from the credit. The internships are what the search found that day.
- **The call:** a real call in the browser through the voice server: NVIDIA Parakeet hears, Magpie speaks, Nemotron 3.5 Lightning talks, and Juno's own agent read the syllabus for the midterm ("Let me check" is Juno's own). The edit trims the waiting (the panel still shows "Answered in 7.8 s" and 2.9 s) and the pauses Magpie leaves between sentences on the server's CPU, and mutes the greeting under the narration. The cut-in is real: the caller starts talking while Juno is mid-sentence, and Juno stops. Diego's half of the call is a stand-in voice played into the page's microphone, heard by the real call turn by turn. The call went on to set a reminder and say goodbye; the cut ends on the second answer.
- **Voice typing:** NVIDIA Parakeet in the desktop app (NeMo-Speech.cpp) hears you; only the words go to the server, which cleans them up (the "um", and "Thursday. Actually, Wednesday." becomes "Wednesday"). The pill says Private.
- **Meeting notes:** the bot asks every time; the call is heard on the computer by Parakeet; Nemotron writes the notes. Notes don't tell voices apart yet, so in the meeting people say whose task is whose; the notes then name them right. The voices of Maya, Sam and Diego are stand-ins.
- **The desktop scenes** are the real desktop app (Electron) on Linux, recorded at 2× on a made-up desktop: a compose window and a video call window drawn for the video (`desktop/scene.cjs`), with the app's speech heard through a test microphone. The app runs the same way on a Mac or a PC.
- **What's hidden while recording:** the site's "save your account" nudges, which only guest accounts see.
- **Open source:** Apache 2.0.

## If Diego records his own narration

Read `vo/lines.json` top to bottom in one take (a quiet room, a hand's width from the mic, a short pause between sentences), save it as a WAV, and `README.md` has the commands that cut it into sentences and place them. The cut's timing allows for a slightly slower read; much slower and a beat gets a little longer (the assembler says where lines would overlap).
