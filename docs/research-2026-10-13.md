# Build-a-bot: research and a sharper version of the idea (2026-10-13)

Status: research and recommendation only. Nothing built. Everything under "Verdict", "Refined concept" and "Recommendation" is Claude's proposal, not a decision by Diego.

**Diego's idea (2026-10-13):** "Build-a-bot ... allows your everyday person to build their own agent. They choose what model they want it to run on (claude, codex, kimi, any open weight model), how they want to interact with it and what tools they want it to use (messages, email, slack), what it has access to (internet, other things), and whether it's always on, requires server, or only on when their computer's open."

**Method:** four research agents (market, channels, models and runtimes, safety and name) worked from dated primary sources on 2026-10-13. Claude then re-checked the claims the recommendation leans on: the Telegram Bot API changelog, Anthropic's Claude Code legal page (read verbatim), OpenAI's Sign in with ChatGPT docs, the USPTO opposition record, TechCrunch's 2026-10-10 roundup, OpenAI Dots, Meta Muse, current Claude prices, and a DNS test on Diego's network. UNVERIFIED marks facts that could not be confirmed as current.

## Verdict

Real demand, crowded at both ends, open in the middle.
- Since June every big lab has shipped an always-on personal agent locked to its own model.
- 22 funded startups put an agent in your texts, and none lets you pick the model.
- Open-source agents (OpenClaw, Hermes) give full choice but are hard to set up and unsafe by default.

Nobody combines the four choices with an experience a non-technical person can trust. To win that slot the idea needs four changes:
1. Start from the job instead of the parts.
2. Make "where it runs" automatic.
3. Enforce safety in code.
4. Pick a different name.

### Where it runs

Every channel except the local relays needs an always-on cloud endpoint. So the honest design is:
- a thin cloud front door that receives everything;
- the work runs on the user's computer when it's awake, and in the cloud when it isn't.

## Safety: what went wrong in 2026

The damage came from product design, not models:
- open control panels;
- agents holding full logins while reading strangers' email and web pages;
- an open skill marketplace;
- rules that lived only in the prompt;
- no remote stop;
- messages sent to people the owner never approved.

More context:
- OpenClaw has 722 published advisories since January (14 critical, 249 high), and its security policy assumes "one trusted operator".
- Human approval alone fails: testers caught 13.6% of dangerous commands (Anthropic, 08-07).

**Minimum to ship, ranked:**
1. **Rules in code, outside the model.** Once a run has read anything the owner didn't write, it cannot send, buy, delete, post or change settings without approval. The rules survive memory compaction and model swaps.
2. **Only the owner gives commands.** The first message to any new recipient needs approval. Rate-limit each contact and cap retries. Label AI-sent messages.
3. **Rare, specific approvals outside the chat** (a push or passkey in the app). No "allow always" for sending or paying.
4. **STOP from every channel and the app**, plus append-only receipts of every action and dollar.
5. **Credentials never inside the agent:** scoped, short-lived, and added only on the way out. Enforce row-level security on our own backend (the Moltbook lesson).
6. **Isolation**, with network access off by default and no public control panel. Local mode authenticates every connection.
7. **Money off by default.** When it's on, use capped tokens tied to one merchant.
8. **Curated, pinned skills only.** No open marketplace: Trail of Bits got malicious skills past every scanner it tested (06-03).
9. **Protected memory and persona.** Store safety rules as policy, not as memory.
10. **Model-side screening as an extra layer**, tested against every model offered. Weaker models get fewer tools.
11. **Identify the agent** to websites and recipients, and keep a breach-notice runbook.

Meta Muse (Sentinel) and ChatGPT Dots (auto-review) already ship versions of items 1 to 4 for their own agents. Safety is table stakes against the labs; it only sets us apart from OpenClaw and its hosts.

## Rules that apply (not legal advice)

- **State companion and conversational chatbot laws:** California SB 243 (in force 01-01; users can sue for at least $1,000 per violation), New York GBL Art. 47, and Washington, Colorado and Oregon from 2027. The baseline:
  - Say it's AI at first contact and every 3 hours.
  - Never claim to be human, even inside a custom persona.
  - Publish a crisis protocol with 988 referral.
- **Launch 18+ with real age checks.** The amended COPPA rule has applied since 04-22, and California SB 1119 (signed 09-10) adds duties for minors.
- **Texting other people:** if the bot texts anyone besides its owner, the platform can be the legal sender under the TCPA (FCC 15-72). The user should start or approve each send.
- **Platform terms:**
  - Apple's iCloud terms bar automated access to iMessage.
  - WhatsApp bans general-purpose AI providers.
  - Google forbids training on Gmail data.
  - Slack forbids training LLMs on its data.
- **EU users:** AI Act Art. 50 disclosure has applied since 08-02. Alternatively, block the EU at launch.

## The name

Medium-high risk as a product or company name; fine as a lowercase tagline ("build your own bot").
- **Build-A-Bear has already won this fight.** It opposed Trendmasters' BUILD A BOT application (toys, serial 75453504; opposition 91113418, filed 1999-03-12). The board sustained the opposition on 2004-09-13 and the application was abandoned (ttabvue.uspto.gov, re-checked 10-13).
- **It polices the pattern.** Build-A-Bear filed 30+ oppositions or cancellations against BUILD-A- names from 1999 to 2012, with none found since 2013 (research agent; not re-checked).
- **The name is already in use:**
  - buildabot.ai is a live no-code AI chatbot builder (Xill LLC).
  - build-a-bot.com is for sale at $3,195.
  - SAINTCON 2026 runs a "Build-a-Bot Workshop" pitched as "Think 'Build-a-Bear,' but you leave with your own AI agent stack".
- **It would be hard to own anyway.** The phrase is descriptive: Automation Anywhere's BUILD-A-BOT only made the Supplemental Register and was cancelled 2025-08-22.
- **A toy-sounding name draws kids**, and the product must be 18+.
- **Directions:** lead with Cookbook ("make your own agent" inside Cookbook), or use an invented word. The research agent quick-checked "Botwright" and "Botlery" and found no US marks. Any name needs a real clearance search.

## Refined concept (proposal)

**Pitch:** "Make your own assistant for one job in five minutes. It lives in your texts, runs on the AI you already pay for, keeps working while your laptop sleeps, never sends, spends or deletes without your tap, and it's yours to take anywhere."

| Diego's choice | What the person sees | Default | Under the hood |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model | "Use the AI you already pay for", "Included", or "Private" | Their ChatGPT or Claude if they have one | ChatGPT via Sign in with ChatGPT. Claude via their own Claude Code. Included: Haiku 5.5 or GPT-6 Luna, about $4 to $5 a month of tokens. Private: a local model (32 GB) |
| Channel | "Where do you want to text it?" | Telegram for tinkerers, iMessage for iPhone people, plus its own email address | Chat SDK handlers; two iMessage vendors |
| Tools and access | "What can it touch?": Look only / Ask me first / Trusted with limits | Look only | The ranked safety list above |
| Runtime | Not a choice. One honest status line: working on your Mac, or your Mac is asleep so it's running in the cloud | Hybrid | Cloud front door, plus the Bridge, plus a Claude routine or the included brain |

Two things the original list didn't have:
- **The job and the personality are the bear.** Every bot starts from a job template (inbox, reminders, errands, family logistics, studying) with the four choices pre-filled. Proactive check-ins, such as a morning brief, are what keep people using it.
- **Ownership.** The bot is a portable recipe (persona, skills, memory, permissions) that exports, moves to another brain or host, and can be shared for others to clone. This answers the labs killing their builder formats. It also matches Diego's 2026-09-29 stance that a personal AI should be about you, and it is the consumer side of the Learning Loop's replication graph.

## Recommendation (proposal)

1. **No separate company and no product code before the hackathon (due 10-30) and finals.** Treat Build-a-bot as Cookbook's single-player front door, and as the lead candidate for the Jan 8 decision in the 10-07 venture review: if Cookbook has fewer than 3 paying teams then, this is the pivot that reuses the engine. A bot for one person needs no teammates to show up, which is where Cookbook stalls (1 of 21 recent outside signups came back).
2. **After 10-30, run a 3-week concierge test.**
   - **Who:** 5 non-developers Diego already knows (family, roommates, club members; no cold outreach).
   - **What:** each names one chore they would hand off.
   - **How:** hand-build each bot on their own OpenClaw or Hermes instance (about $6 to $30 a month each), signed in to their own ChatGPT. Use Telegram, or iMessage through a gateway.
   - **Limits:** "look only" mode, with no sending, no money, and not their main inbox.
   - **Brain question:** offer each the choice of brain and note whether they care.
3. **Gates at week 3:**
   - At least 3 of 5 still text it weekly.
   - At least 2 ask for a second job.
   - At least 2 say they would pay $5 to $10 a month, knowing Muse is free.

   What we learn: which job sticks, whether "choose your model" matters to non-developers, and price. If it doesn't matter, the four choices are a tinkerer feature, and the first market is OpenClaw-curious tinkerers on Telegram and Discord.
4. **Leave the hackathon entry as it is.** It already shows the hybrid: an open-weight brain, a phone channel, a cloud fallback.

## Open decisions for Diego

1. Separate venture, or Cookbook's single-player front door? (Proposal: front door.)
2. First person: tinkerers who want choice, or everyday iPhone users who want a job done? (Proposal: let the test decide; lean toward tinkerers and students, who already pay for or get free ChatGPT and Claude.)
3. An included brain means Cookbook pays for inference (about $4 to $5 per user per month on Haiku 5.5), which conflicts with the 2026-09-02 rule. It's needed only for people without a plan.
